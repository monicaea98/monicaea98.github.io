description: >
  Operational call center data from a <strong>virtual telephone service</strong> was cleaned, transformed, 
  and analyzed to evaluate <strong>operator performance</strong> and identify patterns of 
  <strong>service inefficiency</strong>. The analysis focused on key performance indicators such as 
  <strong>missed call rate</strong>, <strong>average waiting time</strong>, and 
  <strong>outgoing call activity</strong>.

  <strong>Exploratory data analysis</strong> revealed large variability in call duration and workload 
  distribution across operators, highlighting potential <strong>performance imbalances</strong>. 
  Behavioral thresholds were defined using data percentiles to classify inefficiency risk based on 
  <strong>low outbound activity</strong>, <strong>high missed call percentages</strong>, and 
  <strong>long customer wait times</strong>.

  An <strong>operator inefficiency scoring model</strong> was developed to flag operators meeting 
  multiple risk conditions. Statistical hypothesis testing confirmed that 
  <strong>inefficient operators have significantly higher waiting times</strong>, validating waiting time 
  as a strong indicator of service performance issues.

  Findings informed <strong>data-driven operational recommendations</strong>, including targeted training, 
  workload redistribution, and implementation of <strong>real-time performance monitoring dashboards</strong> 
  to improve response times, reduce missed calls, and enhance overall customer service quality.
