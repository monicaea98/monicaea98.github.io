description: >
  User behavior data from an <strong>A/B test of an e-commerce checkout funnel</strong> was cleaned, validated, 
  and analyzed to evaluate the impact of a <strong>new recommendation system</strong> on 
  <strong>conversion rates</strong> across key funnel stages. The analysis focused on user activity within 
  the first <strong>14 days after registration</strong>.

  <strong>Exploratory data analysis</strong> and data validation steps ensured 
  <strong>experiment integrity</strong>, including removal of users assigned to multiple groups, 
  verification of <strong>balanced activity levels</strong>, and checks for 
  <strong>external marketing campaign interference</strong>.

  Funnel performance was measured across <strong>login</strong>, <strong>product page views</strong>, 
  <strong>cart additions</strong>, and <strong>purchases</strong>. Statistical hypothesis testing 
  using <strong>proportion z-tests</strong> revealed that the new funnel increased 
  <strong>mid-funnel engagement</strong> (product page → cart) but did not significantly improve 
  <strong>final purchase conversion</strong>.

  Additional analysis uncovered <strong>event tracking inconsistencies</strong> in the 
  cart-to-purchase stage, highlighting the importance of <strong>data quality in experimentation</strong>. 
  The findings informed recommendations to refine tracking, rerun the experiment, and prioritize 
  <strong>data-driven optimization of the checkout experience</strong>.
