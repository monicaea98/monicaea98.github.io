description: >
  Customer data from a <strong>subscription-based gym business</strong> was cleaned and analyzed to understand 
  <strong>customer behavior</strong> and predict <strong>churn risk</strong>. 
  <strong>Exploratory data analysis</strong> and <strong>feature engineering</strong> were used to evaluate 
  <strong>contract length</strong>, <strong>visit frequency</strong>, <strong>group class participation</strong>, 
  <strong>additional service spending</strong>, and <strong>promotion channels</strong>.

  A <strong>machine learning churn prediction model</strong> was developed to estimate the probability of 
  customers leaving in the following month, while <strong>customer segmentation (clustering)</strong> identified 
  distinct behavioral groups with different levels of retention risk.

  Key <strong>business insights</strong> revealed that <strong>longer contracts</strong>, 
  <strong>partner and referral promotions</strong>, and <strong>active participation in group sessions</strong> 
  significantly increase retention, while <strong>low attendance</strong>, 
  <strong>short-term contracts</strong>, and <strong>new memberships</strong> are strong indicators of churn risk.

  Results informed <strong>data-driven retention strategies</strong>, including 
  <strong>targeted campaigns for high-risk segments</strong>, 
  <strong>engagement incentives to increase gym visits</strong>, and 
  <strong>loyalty programs for long-term members</strong>, helping shift the business from reactive churn management 
  to <strong>proactive customer retention</strong>.
